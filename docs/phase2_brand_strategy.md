## The Brand Team's Internal Mandate

> "A brand is a feeling that logic cannot explain."
> 
> 1IMP must feel like the moment before a great conversation. The quiet confidence of someone who has prepared. The electricity of recognition.

Our job in Phase 2 is not to design a logo.  
Our job is to define the emotional contract between 1IMP and every person who encounters it.

---

## 1. Brand Philosophy

### The Core Feeling We Are Selling

When a candidate uses 1IMP, they should feel:

1. **Seen** — *"This represents who I actually am, not just what I've done"*
2. **Confident** — *"I am proud to share this"*
3. **Ready** — *"I have done everything I can to show up well"*

When a recruiter receives a 1IMP link, they should feel:

1. **Intrigued** — *"This is different from everything else in my inbox"*
2. **Respected** — *"This candidate took this seriously"*
3. **Efficient** — *"I understood this person in under 10 seconds"*

**The brand must serve both feelings simultaneously.**  
This is the design challenge at the heart of 1IMP.

## 2. Brand Naming Deep Dive

### 1IMP

**Pronounced**: One Impression

**Why this name works:**

| Dimension | Analysis |
|-----------|----------|
| **Memorability** | "1IMP" is typographically unusual — it stands out in a list, an inbox, a URL |
| **Pronunciation** | "One Impression" is natural and immediately conveys the mission |
| **Domain availability** | 1imp.io / 1imp.co — short, clean, professional |
| **Social handles** | @1IMP — stands out on every platform |
| **Scalability** | As a number + word hybrid, it reads internationally — works in 50+ languages |
| **Brand voice** | The name itself is bold, minimal, and numerical — aligns with the design philosophy |

**Naming risks and mitigations:**

- *Risk: "IMP" as a negative connotation (devil/mischief)* → Mitigated by always pronouncing "One Impression" in voice contexts and using full wordmark alongside logomark
- *Risk: Number prefix feels startup-gimmicky* → Mitigated by premium design execution — the quality of the product recontextualizes the name

## 4. Brand Pillars

### Pillar 1: Clarity
*Cut through the noise. The best first impression is the clearest one.*  
Everything in the product and brand must communicate immediately. No jargon. No complexity. No confusion. A recruiter should understand the candidate in 10 seconds, and a candidate should understand 1IMP in 10 seconds.

### Pillar 2: Confidence
*We are not timid. We do not hedge. We make a statement.*  
1IMP has a point of view. The PDF resume is outdated. The future of professional discovery is here. The brand speaks with quiet authority, never arrogance, but always certainty.

### Pillar 3: Craft
*Every pixel is intentional. Every word earns its place.*  
The product is someone's professional reputation. That is sacred. We treat every detail with the seriousness of a master craftsman. Users should sense the craft before they can articulate it.

### Pillar 4: Humanity
*We are not building HR software. We are helping people be seen.*  
At the center of every feature is a human being trying to change their life. The brand must always feel warm, not clinical. Intelligent, not cold. Powerful, not overwhelming.

## 5. Visual Identity System

### 5.1 Color Philosophy

The color palette must communicate:
- **Premium** — not startup-bright
- **Confidence** — not aggressive
- **Clarity** — not minimal to the point of emptiness
- **Modern** — not dated

**Palette Decision: Dark with Luminous Accent**

We reject: Pure black + blue (too corporate), White + green (too SaaS-generic), Colorful gradients (too consumer/gaming)

We choose: **Deep Ink backgrounds with a single precise accent — a warm, electric tone that feels like intelligence lit from within.**

---

**Why this palette:**
- `#0A0A0F` (Void) is not pure black — it has a barely perceptible blue-violet undertone that makes it feel premium, not heavy

- `#F0A05A` (Signal) provides warmth against the cool dark system — it is used sparingly on CTAs so it always commands attention

### 5.2 Typography

**Typography Philosophy**: Type is not decoration. In a product about professional first impressions, type IS the product. Every typographic decision must communicate precision, intelligence, and humanity simultaneously.

**Selected Font Stack:**

```
DISPLAY TYPEFACE — "Syne"
Source: Google Fonts (free, variable font)
Usage: Hero headlines, section titles, large numbers
Weight: 600–800
Character: Geometric, architectural, modern — feels handcrafted despite being sans-serif
Why: Syne has a subtle irregularity that makes it feel designed, not default

BODY TYPEFACE — "Inter"
Source: Google Fonts (free, variable font)
Usage: All body text, UI labels, paragraphs
Weight: 300–600
Character: Hyper-legible, neutral, professional
Why: The most-used interface font in premium SaaS — familiar enough to be invisible

MONOSPACE TYPEFACE — "JetBrains Mono"
Source: Google Fonts (free)
Usage: Stats, code, technical data, profile analytics numbers
Weight: 400–500
Character: Clean, technical, precise
Why: Numbers in JetBrains Mono feel like data, not decoration
```

### 5.3 Logo Concept

**Logo Philosophy**: The logomark must work at 16px (favicon) and at 480px (hero). It must be immediately recognizable, not require explanation, and feel premium at every size.

**Concept: The "1" Mark**

The primary logomark is a stylized **"1"** — but not a standard numeral.

- The "1" is constructed with variable-width strokes — thick at the top, refined at the base
- The crossbar of the "1" is replaced with a subtle horizontal sweep that suggests forward motion — a visual metaphor for "moving forward, getting noticed"
- The angle of the stroke suggests a person confidently stepping forward

**Logomark Variants:**

```
Primary Logomark    — "1IMP" horizontal lockup, Syne 700
Secondary Lockup    — Stacked: "1" mark above "IMP"
Monogram            — "1" mark alone (for favicon, app icons)
Reversed            — White on Impression (#7C6AF7) for badge contexts
```

**Logo Color Applications:**

```
On Dark Background  — #F0EFF8 (Primary text white)
On Light Background — #0A0A0F (Void black)
Badge Version       — #FFFFFF on Impression gradient background
Monochrome          — Single color only (no gray)
```

> **Note for execution**: The logo will be designed as SVG in the UI phase. The concept above guides the design brief for the Design System phase.

### 5.5 Motion Philosophy

**Motion Principles:**

1. **Physics-informed**: Every animation must feel like it obeys gravity and momentum. Nothing should feel arbitrary.
2. **Purposeful**: Motion communicates meaning, not decoration. An element entering the screen should tell the user *why* it's appearing.
3. **Quick**: UI micro-interactions cap at 200ms. Page transitions cap at 400ms. Never make users wait for beauty.
4. **Subtle by default**: The best animation is the one users don't consciously notice — they only notice when it's absent.

**Easing Curves:**

```
Enter       cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0)   — Elements entering viewport
Exit        cubic-bezier(0.4, 0.0, 1.0, 1.0)   — Elements leaving viewport
Emphasis    cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0)   — Drawing attention
Standard    cubic-bezier(0.4, 0.0, 0.2, 1.0)   — Default transitions

Duration Reference:
  Micro     100ms   — Hover states, checkbox toggles
  Short     200ms   — Button state changes, tooltips
  Medium    300ms   — Card expansions, drawer opens
  Long      400ms   — Page transitions, modal entrances
  Feature   600ms   — Hero animations, onboarding reveals
```

## 6. Voice & Tone

### Brand Voice Attributes

| Attribute | What It Means | What It Is NOT |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|
| **Confident** | We state things as facts. We don't hedge with "might" or "could help" | Arrogant, dismissive, preachy |
| **Human** | We speak to people, not roles. "You" not "Users." "Hiring managers" not "talent stakeholders" | Warm to the point of unprofessional |
| **Precise** | Every word earns its place. We cut adjectives that don't add meaning | Cold, robotic, technical |
| **Ambitious** | We believe in the future we're building. The language reflects that belief | Hyperbolic, vague, over-promised |
| **Empathetic** | We understand that job seeking is emotionally difficult. We acknowledge that | Pitying, condescending |

## 7. Brand Identity: Dos and Don'ts

### Brand Dos ✅
- Always use the full name "1IMP" in headlines (never "1imp" or "1 IMP")
- Use "impression" as the primary product noun: "your impression," "share your impression"
- Pair the logomark with generous whitespace — never crowd it
- Use photography of real people in real professional contexts — authentic, unposed
- Use motion sparingly — every animation must earn its place
- Use the Impression accent color for one thing per screen — never compete with itself

### Brand Don'ts ❌
- Never call it a "resume builder" or "video resume" in any context
- Never use "AI-powered" as a badge or feature claim — it is assumed
- Never show the product in mockups that look like HR software screenshots
- Never use more than 2 colors in an illustration element
- Never use stock photography that looks like stock photography
- Never write in passive voice: "Impressions are made" → "Make your impression"
- Never use gradients that span more than 2 colors
- Never use shadows as the primary depth mechanism — use layering and opacity

## 8. Photography & Illustration Direction

### Photography
- **Subject**: Real professionals in real moments — a conversation, a presentation, a moment of focus
- **Lighting**: High contrast, dramatic, cinematic — not corporate stock
- **Diversity**: Rigorously diverse across race, gender, age, body type, industry
- **Color treatment**: Slight cool color grade to harmonize with dark brand palette
- **Avoid**: Handshakes, pointing at laptops, people smiling at cameras, staged "diversity" shoots

### Illustration
- **Style**: Geometric, architectural — flat with purposeful depth
- **Palette**: Strictly within brand color system
- **Subject**: Abstract representations of concepts: attention, connection, motion, emergence
- **Usage**: Hero sections, empty states, onboarding steps
- **Avoid**: Character illustrations with faces — these age poorly and can alienate
