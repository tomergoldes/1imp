# 1IMP — Product Principles & Brand Language

---

## PRODUCT PRINCIPLE #1 — The Interview Test

> Every feature that is built must answer one question:
> **"Will this measurably increase the candidate's chance of getting noticed and receiving an interview?"**
> If the answer is no, the feature should not exist.

### Feature Validation Framework

Every new feature must pass all 8 criteria before development begins:

| Criterion | Question |
|-----------|---------|
| **User Value** | How does this directly help the candidate get noticed or get hired? |
| **Business Value** | How does this drive conversion, retention, or virality? |
| **Technical Complexity** | S/M/L/XL — is the build cost proportionate to the impact? |
| **Interview Conversion Impact** | By how much does this increase interview callbacks? Estimate %. |
| **Recruiter Engagement Impact** | Does this make the recruiter spend more time on the profile? |
| **Success Metrics** | Which specific numbers will prove this feature is working? |
| **Risks** | What can go wrong? What could this hurt? |
| **Future Scalability** | Does this compound over time or is it a dead end? |

## BRAND LANGUAGE — The 1IMP Vocabulary

> *"The company that names the thing controls the category."*
> Category creation starts with language. We do not describe features. We name experiences.

### Official Term Glossary

| Term | Definition | Do NOT say |
|------|-----------|------------|
| **First Impression** | The complete 1IMP product — the total package a candidate sends to a recruiter | "Resume", "Profile", "Video resume" |
| **Career Profile** | The candidate's personal page (1imp.io/name) — the public-facing destination | "Profile page", "Portfolio", "Landing page" |
| **Career Pitch** | The 60-second video — coached, scripted, one-take | "Video resume", "Video introduction", "Elevator pitch video" |
| **Recruiter Card** | The compact, speed-optimized view a recruiter sees first | "Summary card", "Preview card" |
| **AI Summary** | The AI-generated professional narrative (3-4 sentences) | "Bio", "About me", "Description" |
| **Impression Score** | The proprietary quality score (1-100) visible only to the candidate | "Profile completeness", "Score" |
| **Impression Link** | The shareable URL to a candidate's Career Profile | "Profile link", "Resume link" |

### Voice Rules

- ❌ "Upload your video resume" → ✅ "Record your Career Pitch"
- ❌ "Your profile is complete" → ✅ "Your First Impression is ready"
- ❌ "Share your profile" → ✅ "Share your Impression"
- ❌ "View profile" → ✅ "View their Impression"
- ❌ "Your resume" → ✅ "Your career story"
- ❌ "Job seekers" → ✅ "Professionals" or "Candidates"

### Why This Matters

When LinkedIn launched, they didn't call it "an online resume." They called it a "professional network."
When Airbnb launched, they didn't call hosts "landlords." They called them "hosts."
When Figma launched, they didn't call designs "files." They called them "designs" with a collaborative layer.

**Language is positioning. Positioning is defensibility. Defensibility is moat.**

By naming our category uniquely — First Impression, Career Pitch, Recruiter Card — we:
1. Make competitors look like they're copying our language when they follow
2. Train users to think in our terms (high switching cost)
3. Create PR-able, memorable vocabulary that spreads
4. Signal to the market that we are a new category, not a feature
